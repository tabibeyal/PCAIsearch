# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two overlapping audiences:

- **Lay practitioners** — asking questions like "what did the Buddha say about anger?" They want clear, trustworthy answers grounded in real suttas, not paraphrases or summaries. No Pāḷi fluency required.
- **Scholars and serious students** — comparing teachings across nikāyas, tracing specific doctrinal threads, verifying citations. They need precision and source fidelity above all else.

Both groups use this as a contemplative or scholarly instrument, not a product or social platform. The primary task on any screen is: find the passage that answers my question.

## Product Purpose

An AI-powered search engine for the Pali Canon (DN, MN, SN, AN, and Khuddaka Nikāya selections). Users ask natural language questions; the tool surfaces actual sutta passages and synthesizes an answer grounded in retrieved citations.

Core constraint: every answer must cite real passages. Hallucinating sutta numbers or paraphrasing source text without grounding is a failure state, not an edge case.

Success looks like: a practitioner or scholar trusts the result enough to cite it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, scholarly, ancient.

Quiet authority — the interface should feel like a knowledgeable, unhurried guide who has read the whole Canon. Not a product, not an institution, not a wellness experience. A contemplative instrument.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / ChatGPT clone** — dark sidebar, white chat bubbles, blue or purple accents. The visual language of "AI assistant" is exactly what this is not.
- **Religious institution site** — ornate temple imagery, decorative golds, incense-and-candle aesthetics. The texts are ancient; the interface should not perform that.
- **Academic database (JSTOR-style)** — dense, utilitarian, no visual hierarchy. Filing-cabinet UX drives away practitioners and disrespects the material.
- **Wellness / mindfulness app** — pastel gradients, rounded everything, overtly "peaceful" branding. Calm is the tone; "calm" as an aesthetic gesture is noise.

## Design Principles

1. **Accuracy as aesthetic** — the design should feel as grounded and reliable as the texts it searches. Anything decorative that cannot be defended against "does this help the user trust the result?" should be cut.
2. **Reverence without ritual** — honor the source material through typographic care and deliberate whitespace, not through religious imagery or ornamentation.
3. **Tool, not temple** — clean and functional with depth; scholarly authority without institutional stiffness or product-dashboard busyness.
4. **Silence before speech** — the interface waits for the user's question; it does not perform. Load states, empty states, and transitions should feel unhurried, not eager.
5. **Every answer must be earned** — design choices at the result level should reinforce grounding in real citations: passage text presented with weight and care, sutta IDs legible and linkable, synthesis clearly sourced.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Keyboard navigation for all interactive elements. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Sufficient contrast at all text sizes. No color-only indicators.
